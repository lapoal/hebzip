The old village sits beside a quiet river. Every morning the baker opens his small shop, and the smell of fresh bread fills the street. Children walk to school along the river, and the teacher waits for them at the gate.
In the spring the fields turn green again. The farmers work from early morning until the sun goes down. They plant corn, beans and wheat, and they hope for gentle rain.
The library is the oldest building in the village. It has a red door, tall windows and many shelves of books. People come to read, to write letters, and to talk quietly with their friends.
A small cat lives in the library. Nobody knows where it came from. The cat sleeps on the warm windows in the afternoon, and at night it walks between the shelves like a little guard.
When it rains, the river grows wide and fast. The bridge over the river is made of stone, and it is very old. The people of the village say that the bridge is older than the library.
My grandfather rode a bicycle to the market every week. The bicycle was black and heavy, and it had a basket in front. He bought apples, cheese, bread and coffee, and he always came home before noon.
The market opens on Monday and on Thursday. Farmers bring vegetables, eggs and milk. The baker brings bread and cakes. Children like the market because there is music and there are sweets.
In the summer the days are long and warm. Families eat dinner in the garden and talk until the stars come out. The cat sits on the wall and watches the birds.
In the autumn the leaves turn yellow and red, and the wind blows them along the street. The farmers bring the harvest home, and the village has a small festival with music, bread and apples.
In the winter it is cold and dark. Snow covers the fields and the roofs. The river is slow and grey. People stay at home, read books from the library, and drink hot tea or coffee by the fire.
The teacher tells the children a story about the river. Long ago, she says, a boat came down the river with a strange letter. Nobody could read the letter, so the people kept it in the library.
Today the letter is still in the library. It lies under glass in a small room. Some people say it is a map. Others say it is a poem. The children think it is a secret message.
A computer now stands in the corner of the library. Young people use it to write, to search, and to send messages to friends in the city. The old librarian does not like the computer, but she helps everyone who asks.
Chocolate is sold in the small shop next to the bakery. It is dark and sweet, and it comes from far away. On holidays the children receive a piece of chocolate after dinner.
Rain is important for the fields. Without rain the corn is small and the wheat is dry. With too much rain the river floods the low fields. The farmers watch the sky every day.
The doctor of the village has a white house near the bridge. She rides a bicycle to visit the sick, and she always carries a small bag with medicine, a notebook and a pencil.
On Sunday the bells ring, and people walk slowly to the square. They meet their neighbours, talk about the weather, and share news about the market, the fields and the river.
"Good morning," says the baker. "Good morning," says the teacher. "Is it going to rain today?" asks the doctor. "I hope so," says the farmer, "the fields are dry."
The children ask the librarian about the letter: "Can we read it?" She smiles and says: "Maybe one day you will." Then she gives them a book about maps and old roads.
The road to the city is long. It goes over the hill, through the forest, and along the river. In the past people walked for two days; now the bus takes two hours.
The forest is dark and green. There are tall trees, small paths and a little lake. In the summer the children swim in the lake, and in the winter they skate on the ice.
My grandmother made bread every Friday. She used flour, water, salt and a little sugar. The kitchen was warm, and the whole house smelled of bread and coffee.
A good story has a beginning, a middle and an end. The teacher says that every person in the village has a story, and that the library is the house of all the stories.
The bicycle is a simple machine. It has two wheels, a frame, a chain and pedals. With a bicycle a person can travel far without noise and without smoke.
The cat is a quiet animal. It sleeps much of the day, and it hunts at night. Cats like warm places, soft beds and fresh milk; they do not like rain or loud music.
Water falls from the clouds as rain or snow. It runs into the river, the river runs into the sea, and the sun lifts the water into the sky again.
The old bridge needs repair. The people of the village collect money, and in the spring the workers come with stone and tools. After the summer the bridge is strong again.
At the end of the day the baker closes his shop, the teacher goes home, the farmers rest, and the cat walks into the library for the night.
