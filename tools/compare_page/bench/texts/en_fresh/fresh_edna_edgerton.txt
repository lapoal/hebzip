Edna Anna Edgerton (May 15, 1893 – January 28, 1956) was an American politician who served in the Connecticut House of Representatives from 1943 to 1949, representing the city of Stamford as a Republican. Born in New York City, Edgerton later moved to Connecticut, where she became involved in civic and social service initiatives. In 1948, she co-founded the Connecticut Intercollegiate State Legislature, an annual mock session of the Connecticut General Assembly for college students.
During her three terms in the Connecticut House, Edgerton focused on legislation concerning health services, education, and child welfare. She introduced a bill establishing the State Commission on Alcoholism and was appointed to serve on it in 1945. The commission developed a program that funded clinics for the treatment of alcoholics. She also worked on legislation to introduce stricter adoption standards, equalize the pay of male and female teachers, and create a state psychiatric facility for children. Edgerton and her husband died in a plane crash in 1956 while traveling in Venezuela.
